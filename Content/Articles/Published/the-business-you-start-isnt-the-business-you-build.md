When I started Complexity Clarified, I wasn't entirely sure what I was building. I had ideas and consultancy seemed like the obvious direction. After decades working in technology and digital transformation, it was a logical place to begin.

But I wasn't particularly committed to the idea...

I've always been entrepreneurial, and my ADHD means I'm rarely short of ideas. Whether that's an advantage or a liability probably depends on which day you ask me.

What I knew was that I wanted to build something of my own but what that something would eventually become was considerably less clear.

Perhaps that's the part of starting a business we don't talk about enough.

### Finding My Feet

What started as an idea for a consultancy gradually became something else - I began writing about my experiences, exploring ideas I'd had for years and experimenting with technology I actually wanted to build.

Somewhere along the way, XIRVIX emerged - what began as a relatively simple idea for business software has already evolved into something considerably more ambitious.

None of this was particularly planned; one idea led to another, and before I knew it, I was juggling two websites, a growing collection of articles and a software platform that I wasn't entirely sure anyone would want.

There have been moments of genuine excitement, plenty of frustration and more than a few occasions when I've wondered what on earth I'm doing.

But perhaps the biggest surprise has been how much I've enjoyed the process itself.

### Still Figuring It Out

I still don't know exactly what Complexity Clarified will become, or where XIRVIX will take me. I'd obviously like both to succeed, although what success actually looks like is something I'm still working out.

Some days I feel like I'm making real progress... other days I wonder whether I'm just finding increasingly elaborate ways to keep myself occupied.

I'm enjoying building something of my own, making decisions without endless meetings and having the freedom to follow an idea simply because I think it's worth exploring... perhaps in a year's time I'll look back and wonder how I ended up doing something completely different from what I'd originally imagined.

Given how things have gone so far, I wouldn't be remotely surprised.
