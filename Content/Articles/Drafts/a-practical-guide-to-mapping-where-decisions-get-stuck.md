## Where Decisions Get Stuck

Most organisations don't have a shortage of decisions to make:

- Priorities need to be agreed 
- Projects need approval 
- Suppliers need selecting 
- Budgets need allocating
- Problems need resolving

The difficulty is often not making the decision itself; it's getting the decision to the point where it can be made.

A relatively simple decision can pass through multiple people, meetings and approval stages before anything happens. Information is requested, recommendations are challenged, additional stakeholders become involved and decisions sometimes travel backwards because something was missing earlier in the process.

Individually, each step can appear perfectly reasonable but collectively, they can turn a decision that should take hours into one that takes days or weeks and because much of that time is spent waiting rather than actively doing anything, the problem can be surprisingly difficult to see.

### Start with a real decision

The easiest way to understand how decisions actually move through an organisation is not to map “decision-making” as an abstract process.

Simply pick one - it could be approving a campaign, selecting a supplier, prioritising a development request or agreeing a change to an existing process.

Choose something that happens regularly enough to be representative, then identify a recent example and trace what actually happened.

Start with a few simple questions:

- Who initiated the decision?
- What information was needed?
- Who provided that information?
- Who made a recommendation?
- Who needed to approve it?
- How long did each stage take?
- Did the decision ever move backwards?

Don't worry initially about how the process is supposed to work, just map how the decision actually happened.

That difference is often where the complexity starts to become visible.
