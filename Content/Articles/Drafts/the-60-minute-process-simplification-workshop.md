## Part 1 — Before You Start

Most business processes don't become complicated because somebody deliberately designed them that way... they become complicated gradually.

An approval is added because something once went wrong, a spreadsheet appears because the system doesn't quite work. 

Someone creates a workaround; another check is added to make sure the workaround worked.

Eventually, nobody remembers why half of it exists and then somebody suggests automating it but, before you automate a complicated process, there's a simpler question worth asking:

***Does all of this need to happen at all?***

This workshop is designed to answer that question.

In 60 minutes, you'll take one real business process, identify where unnecessary complexity has accumulated, and design a simpler version you can test.

> ***You won't leave with a perfect process...***
> ***...you should leave with a better one.***

### Part 2 - Get the Right People in the Room

Keep the group small; four to seven people is usually enough.

You need people who understand what actually happens, not just what the process says should happen.

Include someone who:

- does the work
- receives or depends on the output
- owns the process or can make decisions about it
- understands the technology involved, where relevant

Most importantly, don't run the workshop entirely with managers - if nobody in the room actually performs the process, you'll probably map the process people think exists.

Choose one process with a clear beginning and end, and bring nothing more complicated than a whiteboard, sticky notes and something to write with.

> ***You don't need specialist software...***
> ***...you need the people who know where the complexity really is.***

### Part 3 - Run the Workshop

Running a workshop shouldn't be complex - simply start with a blank board and map what actually happens today.

Don't map the documented process; ask the people doing the work:

***What happens when a real one of these arrives?***

Then start the clock:

- 0–5 minutes — Define it: Agree where the process starts, where it ends and what it is supposed to achieve.
- 5–15 minutes — Map it: Capture every step, handoff, system, spreadsheet and workaround.
- 15–25 minutes — Find the friction: Mark delays, duplication, approvals, rework and unnecessary handoffs.
- 25–40 minutes — Challenge it: For every step ask, “What would happen if we stopped doing this?”
- 40–50 minutes — Simplify it: Remove, combine or simplify anything that doesn't need to be there.
- 50–57 minutes — Test it: Check the new process against genuine risks, constraints and dependencies.
- 57–60 minutes — Commit: Agree what will change, who owns it and when you'll test it.

Don't finish with a list of twenty-seven improvements and another meeting - just pick the changes that matter and try them.

> ***The aim isn't to make the process diagram prettier...***
> ***...it's to make the process simpler.***
